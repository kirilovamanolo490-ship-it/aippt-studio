# Intake and routing

Use this reference after the request has been recognized as presentation work. It turns a brief into the minimum decision record needed for good editing or composition. Do not show this record as slide copy.

## Build an intent record

Capture only information that changes the deck:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Who will see or present it, and in what occasion? | Sets register, evidence depth, pace, and visual form. |
| What should the audience understand, decide, remember, or do? | Determines the deck story and the message of each page. |
| Is the request a narrow repair, a derivative of an actual template, a new composition, a reconstruction, or a visual alternate? | Prevents a small edit from becoming an unrequested redesign. |
| What sources are authoritative and what may not be added? | Prevents invented claims, facts, images, or conclusions. |
| Which pages or elements are accepted, frozen, or out of scope? | Preserves useful prior work. |
| What reference is binding: content, composition, template, brand, or merely inspiration? | Avoids copying only a palette while losing its visual logic. |
| What can the current host actually edit and verify? | Keeps claims about editability, export, and source access honest. |
| Is there an existing PPT to beautify throughout, or is this a new deck from Word, notes, or other material? | Determines whether page and object preservation or content synthesis is the dominant constraint. |
| Are portfolio examples accessible, and what exactly should be borrowed from them? | Turns useful prior work into a style contract without copying its client facts. |
| Which images are supplied, which need real-source research, and which could be illustrated? | Prevents irrelevant stock photos and fabricated evidence. |

If a source is only a screenshot or visual reference, do not treat it as an authority for factual content. If a document is a content source, do not replace its facts with generic business language.

## Select the modification route

### Exact local revision

Use for requests such as “fix the third page,” “keep the first two pages,” or “repair the overlapping image.”

- Define the target slide and object scope before changing anything.
- Preserve non-target pages, accepted text, navigation, master behavior, and assets unless the request expands scope.
- Make the smallest coherent repair, then inspect the whole changed page and its neighbors.
- When the host can compare before and after state, record the non-target-slide preservation result and its evidence. When it cannot, record not_comparable rather than claiming that unrelated pages were unchanged.
- A clean object-level edit does not prove that the page reads well.

### Template derivative

Use when the user supplies a real deck or template and expects new content in its language.

- Inspect page roles, composition, type hierarchy, image treatment, spacing rhythm, and recurring visual relationships.
- Reuse the template's actual structure where suitable; do not build a generic slide over its colors.
- Match new content to available page roles. Create a deliberate new role when the template has none, and keep it visually related.
- Treat a template's inherited chapter labels or page numbers as meaningful only if they are real navigation or requested structure.

### New composition

Use when there is no usable template or the requested content needs a distinct expression.

- Start with the argument, evidence, audience, and presentation form.
- Establish a limited visual system that carries the story: hierarchy, image role, diagram logic, spacing, and transitions between pages.
- Do not default to a dashboard of cards, ornamental gradients, random labels, or a title-plus-side-caption pattern.
- Let the page message determine density and visual form. A sparse executive conclusion and a teaching procedure should not be forced into the same geometry.
- For one or two pages, place the actual message and evidence directly; do not insert a generic cover, agenda, or conclusion to satisfy a familiar deck shape. For longer work, vary page structures when the kind of evidence changes while keeping one visual system.

Choose an expression from the source relationship before arranging objects. A single distinct claim may take a generous statement layout; an explicit two-sided comparison may take paired fields; an actual order may take a process or timeline; a real picture may lead or support text. Two or three independent points can use editorial columns without pretending they are a comparison. Dense numeric series and exact multi-column values call for a legible chart or table only when the source supplies the complete labels, units, periods, and comparison basis. When it does not, preserve the facts as text and identify the missing structure. Do not repeat one card grid for a problem, a solution, and implementation stages merely because all three contain several items.

When the user explicitly asks for a cinematic or motion-led presentation, turn that direction into a scene and playback plan before arranging a page. Decide what the opening establishes, which subject or evidence persists into the next page, where the view moves closer or changes scale, and what the audience should notice at each reveal. A page with one suitable source image and one short claim may use a single large, uncropped frame with a stable readable text zone; do not invent a photo or apply that treatment to every page. Keep actual source evidence and the intended venue in control: a formal government or academic page may need a restrained static reading even when another part of the deck is theatrical. If no suitable visual is available, record that asset gap and source or illustrate it under the task's permissions before claiming a cinematic result.

Treat native PowerPoint playback and exported video as separate results. A static slide screenshot proves neither transition timing nor animation playback, and an exported movie file must be inspected across representative frames before calling its motion successful. Microsoft warns that Mac PowerPoint movie export omits object animation and may alter transitions; if the requested deliverable is an animated movie, choose an authoring/export route that actually produces those frames instead of assuming native PPTX animation survives the export. Use only effects supported and verified by the current host; do not fill every page with unrelated fly-ins or promise a movie effect merely because it is specified in an internal plan.

Write the proposed page expression and its source reason in the internal page map. On the actual page, inspect text width, reading order, image-to-claim fit, image surface and backing geometry, and how the page leads to its neighbors. A structural or font-size check cannot approve its visual or spoken expression.

### Faithful reconstruction

Use when the reference's composition itself matters.

- Distinguish the reference's visual contract from its factual content.
- Match relative placement, alignment, crop, overlap, typography role, and object relationships before borrowing superficial colors.
- Preserve editable elements when the host makes that possible. If the only supported result is a flattened visual, describe it truthfully.
- Do not silently substitute a generic template for a reconstruction request.

### Visual alternate

Use when the user asks for another style while keeping the content.

- State the proposed shift in visual language and what remains invariant: facts, narrative order, brand, audience, or source evidence.
- Keep the new direction coherent across pages. It must still meet the requested presentation form.
- A visual alternate is not permission to rewrite source facts or discard accepted material.

## Two complete production workflows

### Beautify an existing PPT throughout

When the user supplies a source deck and requests an overall redesign, do not handle it as a sequence of unrelated local edits. Use `visual_alternate` for a new visual direction or `template_derivative` when the existing template must govern the result; list the entire deck as the affected scope.

1. Inventory every source slide's facts, text, charts, tables, images, media, animations, speaker notes, and page role. Mark what must remain byte-for-byte, visually equivalent, editable, or merely factually faithful. If an old slide is weak, its content can be redistributed only when the request permits it and the source-to-result map stays clear.
2. Infer audience and occasion from the request, then select one presentation form and a compatible design grammar below. Treat a supplied reference or portfolio work as a composition contract when the user asks to follow it, not as a palette alone.
3. Plan a deck-wide rhythm: opening, sections, evidence pages, comparison or process pages, and close. Give each source slide a new role and a visible relationship to its neighbors. Vary composition according to the message while preserving a coherent grid, type relationships, image treatment, and navigation.
4. Rebuild page hierarchy and image geometry around the source meaning. Reuse real source photos and charts where they convey identity or evidence; research or generate only the missing visual roles allowed by the source boundary. Do not replace real data with decorative illustration.
5. Review every revised page at presentation scale and scan the entire deck for repeated filler layouts, density jumps, clipped content, mismatched image frames, meaningless numbering, and lost source objects. Verify preservation and native editability only to the extent the host can actually prove them.

### Create a new PPT from Word, notes, data, or a brief

Use `new_composition` unless an actual supplied deck or template controls the design. A document is a source of content, not a slide-by-slide layout instruction.

1. Read the material as a whole. Extract the actual audience-facing claims, names, dates, quantities, tables, images, citations, constraints, and unresolved questions. Distinguish facts from instructions, examples, and background prose.
2. Build a coverage map that connects each important source fact to a planned slide or to a deliberate omission. Do not make one source paragraph equal one slide or invent a conclusion to fill a familiar deck structure.
3. Set the deck story and page-message map. Choose the presentation form from audience and occasion, then use its page roles and visual grammar below. Give dense content the space, split, comparison, diagram, or sequence it needs; do not hide the essential explanation in a narrow small-text rail.
4. Make an asset plan for each page: supplied visual, real sourced visual, generated illustration, editable diagram or chart, or no image. Search and generate conditionally using [visual sourcing](visual-sourcing.md).
5. Create and review the actual pages, including opening, transition, evidence, and closing pages. Recheck all claims and numbers against the source, then inspect whole-page expression and adjacent-page flow.

## Turn portfolio examples into a usable style contract

Use portfolio or prior-work examples only when the user provides them in this task or an approved connected source actually exposes them. The uploaded skill has no inherent access to a private local portfolio. Select examples for the **same audience, occasion, and content role**; an attractive portfolio cover is not automatically a suitable government evidence page. For each chosen example, identify:

- its composition: grid, margins, alignment, negative space, and how objects relate;
- its typographic hierarchy: which message is primary and how supporting facts remain readable;
- its image language: documentary, editorial, illustrative, diagrammatic, or absent;
- its page roles and deck rhythm: what varies, what stays consistent, and why;
- the content and brand details that belong only to that earlier project and must not be copied.

Carry these observations into a short internal design contract for the current deck. A supplied complete reference controls more than color: match its type roles, composition, image boundaries, pacing, and element relationships where the new content permits. If no relevant example is accessible, use the presentation-form guidance below and state that the portfolio was not consulted; do not claim to have learned its style.

## Choose the presentation form

These forms are distinct design grammars, not locked templates or fixed font-size schedules. Within a grammar, content roles still produce different page compositions.

| Form | Narrative and page roles | Layout, imagery, and voice | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Formal government or institution briefing | Responsibility, basis, progress, risk, measure, and next action; explicit chapters and evidence pages. | Formal Chinese or the source language; stable title and evidence hierarchy, orderly alignment, restrained emphasis, authentic institutional and geographic imagery when relevant. | Decorative patriotism, invented official scenes, consumer dashboard cards, unsourced slogans, and theatrical effects. |
| Business report, board update, or strategy deck | Decision or conclusion, comparison, cause, implication, action; separate executive summary from detailed evidence. | Clear claim-led pages, comparable scales and labeled baselines, deliberate chart/table hierarchy, concise wording suitable for a meeting. | Generic “key insight” labels, invented growth claims, dashboards without a decision, and decorative photos unrelated to the business. |
| Competition, proposal, or defense | Problem, insight, solution, mechanism, differentiation, feasibility, proof, and likely questions. | A persuasive progression with one focal argument per page, visible evidence, stronger contrast and pacing where appropriate, sourced real-world visuals or truthful conceptual illustrations. | Loud decoration that outruns proof, tiny evidence relegated beside a giant slogan, and unsupported superiority claims. |
| Academic defense | Question, method, result, interpretation, limits, contribution. | Precise terms, readable figures, complete axes and units, disciplined evidence hierarchy, restrained visual accents. | Marketing exaggeration, ornamental icons replacing methods, and image generation standing in for results. |
| Teaching or training | Goal, example, demonstration, comparison, practice, feedback. | Learner-paced progression, clear diagrams and real examples, hierarchy that makes the lesson visible from a distance. | A wall of document prose, decorative numbered steps, and images that show the wrong action. |
| Technical or product explanation | Mechanism, constraint, architecture, behavior, evidence, and tradeoff. | Editable diagrams where possible; relationships and labels are legible; visuals show actual system behavior and limits. | Anonymous boxes, false process arrows, and generated imagery used as proof of a real product. |
| Premium portfolio or editorial presentation | Real work, authorship, material, case sequence, craft, and outcome. | Spacious editorial rhythm, intentional type contrast, carefully cropped source-faithful imagery, selective full-bleed or open layouts when the material warrants them. | Repeating the same business-card grid, copying another client's work, and visual spectacle that hides the actual project. |
| Ceremony, culture, or public narrative | Occasion, people, chronology, place, feeling, and shared meaning. | Scene-led pacing, meaningful historical or cultural imagery, respectful tone, and transitions that maintain context. | Generic stock emotion, fabricated documentary images, and decorative motifs with no relation to the story. |

When form is uncertain, do not impose a business-consulting voice by default. Use the supplied audience and material, make a conservative plan, and identify the one missing decision that would change the deck materially.

## Set a concrete visual baseline for a new composition

For a new deck without a binding template or complete visual reference, choose one form above and start from a complete page system. The measurements below use a 1920 × 1080 design canvas corresponding to a 13⅓ × 7½ inch slide; **2 canvas pixels are approximately 1 PowerPoint point**. Insets and gaps are canvas pixels; type sizes are points. On another aspect ratio, scale the system with the usable page width and height, then judge the actual page. These numbers establish relationships for a first draft, not a universal minimum or a reason to shrink essential content until it fits.

| Form | Horizontal / vertical inset | Cover / page title / body / caption | Title-to-body / item / column gap | Image : text starting columns | Page rhythm and image treatment |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| General editorial | 112 / 88 | 47 / 35 / 23 / 15 | 46 / 22 / 48 | 1.08 : 1 | Alternate claims, evidence, and meaningful images; keep image edges honest. |
| Formal government | 124 / 82 | 43 / 32 / 22 / 14 | 38 / 18 / 44 | 1 : 1 | Stable evidence hierarchy, restrained emphasis, authentic institutional material. |
| Business decision | 112 / 84 | 44 / 33 / 23 / 14.5 | 38 / 20 / 52 | 1.08 : 1 | Rotate comparison, trend, decision, and action pages around the decision. |
| Competition defense | 104 / 78 | 46 / 34 / 23 / 14 | 42 / 20 / 48 | 0.92 : 1.08 | Change pace at the problem, differentiation, proof, and feasibility turns. |
| Academic defense | 116 / 78 | 43 / 32 / 21 / 13.5 | 40 / 18 / 42 | 1 : 1.05 | Reserve actual room for methods, chart labels, units, and source figures. |
| Teaching or training | 116 / 82 | 46 / 34 / 24 / 15 | 42 / 24 / 44 | 0.92 : 1.08 | One learnable idea per page; keep example and explanation adjacent. |
| Children learning | 104 / 72 | 50 / 37 / 26 / 16 | 36 / 28 / 40 | 1 : 1 | One short concrete idea, large meaningful labels, relevant picture and example. |
| Technical explanation | 108 / 82 | 45 / 33 / 22 / 14 | 42 / 20 / 44 | 0.90 : 1.10 | Show real mechanisms and relationships before decorative interface shapes. |
| Ceremony or public narrative | 126 / 80 | 49 / 35 / 22 / 14 | 40 / 26 / 48 | 1.2 : 0.8 | Let scene scale and silence vary while identity stays coherent. |
| Premium portfolio | 112 / 72 | 44 / 32 / 21 / 13.5 | 34 / 22 / 56 | 1.25 : 0.75 | Let the actual work carry the page; vary editorial crop and whitespace. |

Children learning is selected only for an explicitly young learning audience, not every presentation that mentions children. For a Chinese deck on macOS, use a reliable Chinese sans family such as Hiragino Sans GB or Heiti SC for body text; Songti SC can lead academic or ceremonial headings when it fits the reference. On Windows, Microsoft YaHei UI or Microsoft YaHei is a sensible Chinese sans fallback; on Linux, use an available Noto Sans CJK SC or equivalent. Avenir Next, DIN Alternate, or Arial can provide Latin and numeric contrast where installed, but do not assume a Latin face supplies Chinese glyphs. Use the host's installed fonts and confirm the actual rendering. A supplied brand, template, reference, or explicit font choice takes precedence.

Apply the baseline as a system: establish title and evidence roles, group related elements by a consistent gap rhythm, use a suitable image-to-text relationship, and vary page composition when the message changes. Keep captions tied to their evidence. Do not add a small explanatory rail just because a large headline leaves space. If content is too dense, restructure or split the message before reducing type. When adapting an existing deck, treat these numbers as review questions rather than silently replacing its established visual language.

## Page-message map

Before building, write a compact internal map:

1. Page role in the overall story.
2. One sentence the audience should take away.
3. Evidence, source, or visual that makes the sentence credible.
4. Relationship to the preceding and following page.
5. Constraints: elements to preserve, required content, required speaker notes, or verified data.

A page should not be added only because a conventional deck “usually has” that page. If the material does not support a claim, use an honest question, a limited observation, or omit it.

## Source and preservation rules

- Treat the newest user-provided source and direct feedback as the truth for this task.
- Preserve complete units, names, dates, percentages, comparison bases, and source identities when they matter to meaning.
- Do not turn a source-only request into broad external research without authorization.
- Use connected apps only when they are available in the current surface and permitted by the user and workspace.
- Record uncertainty instead of inventing a fact, missing asset, client identity, or visual approval.
